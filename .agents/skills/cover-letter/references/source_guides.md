## 1. National Careers Service (UK) — covering letter
https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/covering-letter

- Structure: 3–5 paragraphs.
  - Intro: who you are, the job you're applying for, where you found it.
  - Body: why your skills match the role and the employer's values.
  - Extra paragraph (optional): explain gaps, or mention disability + adjustments if you want.
  - End: thanks + how to contact you.
- Research the employer before writing; tailor every letter (never reuse).
- Use STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for examples.
- Professional tone, consistent font with the CV, zero errors.

## 2. University of Michigan Career Center — Cover Letter Resources
https://careercenter.umich.edu/content/cover-letter-resources

- 3–4 paragraphs:
  - P1: introduce yourself, why THIS position/organization; referral name in the first sentence if any.
  - P2–3: connect experiences to the JD with specific academic/work/volunteer examples; include numbers,
    results, accomplishments; mirror the JD's language; do NOT repeat resume facts — focus on key concepts.
  - P4: final statement of interest, thanks, tie in the company's tagline/mission/values.
- Quick tips:
  - ≤ 1 page. Persuasive, show enthusiasm. Emphasize hard AND soft skills.
  - Research via LinkedIn + company website; target the skills the employer seeks.
  - Find a named recipient; else use a title (Hiring Manager, HR Director). Avoid "To Whom It May Concern".
  - Error-free — the letter showcases writing ability.
  - Avoid starting every sentence with "I". Avoid clichés ("...make me an outstanding candidate").
- Formatting: 10–12 pt, same font as resume; 0.5–1" margins; left-justified, start ≤ 2" from top;
  positive language, confident not imposing, concise with supporting detail, active verb voice.
- Email applications: use the letter as the email body with a professional salutation.

## 3. r/datascience — "My Guide To Writing A Killer Cover Letter" (u/ibsurvivors, careerfair.io)
https://www.reddit.com/r/datascience/comments/tag8l5/my_guide_to_writing_a_killer_cover_letter/

Core thesis: a cover letter is a marketing tool, not an autobiography.

Step 1 — Analyze the JD:
- Two parts: "what you'll do" (first bullets are the most important) + "qualifications".
- Highlight bullets you've done; note qualifications you meet; highlight preferred/nice-to-have items you satisfy.
- Qualifications are negotiable — missing a few should not deter you from applying.

Step 2 — Match table:
- Two columns: JD requirement | your matching qualification. Use as many of the JD's exact words as possible
  ("QuickBooks", not "accounting software"). Only the top two matches will be used.

Step 3 — Company research:
- Mission, problem they solve, product, uniqueness vs competitors, homepage values, community programs.
- Founders' interviews and the company blog are good sources. If you actually use their product, say so first.

Step 4 — Letter format:
1. (i) Who you are, what you want, what you believe in — 1–2 sentences, ideally one company-specific detail.
2. (ii) Transition: summary statement ("Over the last 12 months I've generated $X…") + bridge
   ("Three things make me the perfect fit for this position:").
   - Avoid jargon, get specific: "half the words, twice the examples, a few numbers sprinkled in."
3. (iii) Skill & Qualification Match: pick the top 2 requirements; link each to a story theme:
   Leading People · Taking initiative · Affinity for challenging work · Affinity for different/specific work ·
   Dealing with failure · Managing conflict · Driven by curiosity.
4. (iv) Why do you want to work there: two reasons — one values-driven, one industry/topical.
   Template: "Third, I've been following [COMPANY]… The [value] really stands out to me because [reason].
   I also recently read that [topical fact], and this appeals to me because [why]."
5. (v) Conclusion: state what you want and why ("I think you'll find my experience is a really good fit for
   [COMPANY]… I look forward to hearing back.") + sign-off.
- Always write from scratch; five tailored applications beat fifty generic ones.
