# Harvard Action Verbs Reference Library

Action verbs bring your bullet points alive, demonstrate ownership, and convey active initiative rather than passive compliance. 

Never begin a bullet point with passive phrases like *"Responsible for"*, *"Assisted with"*, *"Worked on"*, or *"Helped in"*. Instead, select a specific verb from one of the eight functional categories below.

---

## 1. Leadership & Management
Use these when describing leading teams, driving projects, making strategic decisions, or taking initiative:

| Verb | Verb | Verb | Verb |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accomplished** | **Achieved** | **Administered** | **Analyzed** |
| **Assigned** | **Attained** | **Chaired** | **Consolidated** |
| **Contracted** | **Coordinated** | **Delegated** | **Developed** |
| **Directed** | **Earned** | **Evaluated** | **Executed** |
| **Handled** | **Headed** | **Impacted** | **Improved** |
| **Increased** | **Led** | **Mastered** | **Orchestrated** |
| **Organized** | **Oversaw** | **Planned** | **Predicted** |
| **Prioritized** | **Produced** | **Proved** | **Recommended** |
| **Regulated** | **Reorganized** | **Reviewed** | **Scheduled** |
| **Spearheaded** | **Strengthened** | **Supervised** | **Surpassed** |

### Example Transformation:
- **Weak**: *Was responsible for leading the student association marketing team.*
- **Strong**: *Spearheaded 6-person student marketing committee, orchestrating campus-wide awareness campaigns that boosted event attendance by 42%.*
